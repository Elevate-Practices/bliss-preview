What to Do in a Dental Emergency: Steps to Save Your Smile

Dental emergencies tend to happen at the worst times and can cause real pain and worry. Knowing what to do in the first few minutes can protect your tooth and your comfort. If you need help now, call us at the number at the bottom of this page.

What counts as a dental emergency?

Common emergencies include a severe toothache, a knocked-out or loose tooth, a broken or cracked tooth, a lost crown or filling, swelling in the face or gums, and cuts to the gums, cheeks or tongue. Quick action often makes a big difference.

Step 1: Stay calm and look at what happened

Take a breath and check what you can see. Rinse your mouth gently with warm water. Note where it hurts and whether anything is broken, loose or bleeding.

Step 2: Call your dentist right away

Call us as soon as you can. We make room for emergencies, offer same-day visits, and can tell you what to do on the phone until you get here.

Step 3: Care for the tooth and manage pain

  • Knocked-out adult tooth: Pick it up by the crown, not the root. Rinse it gently without scrubbing. If you can, place it back in the socket, or keep it in a cup of milk, and get to a dentist quickly, ideally within an hour.
  • Broken tooth: Save any pieces and rinse your mouth with warm water.
  • Toothache or swelling: Use a cold compress on the outside of your cheek and an over-the-counter pain reliever as directed. Don't put aspirin directly on the gums or tooth.
  • Bleeding: Press gently with clean gauze.

If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, or swelling that is spreading, go to an emergency room.

Step 4: Get ready for your visit

Bring your insurance information and a list of your medications if you can. Our office is on Boat Club Road in Lake Country. See our hours and directions.

Step 5: Follow your aftercare instructions

After treatment, follow the instructions we give you for medication, eating and follow-up visits so you heal well. Learn more about our same-day dental emergency care.

FAQ

Quick answers.

Can I get a same-day emergency dental appointment?

We offer same-day emergency visits. Call us as early as you can so we can fit you in.

What should I do if a tooth is knocked out?

Hold it by the crown, rinse it gently, try to put it back in the socket or keep it in milk, and see a dentist right away, ideally within an hour.

When should I go to the emergency room instead?

If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, spreading facial swelling, or a serious injury to your head or jaw, go to an emergency room.
