Understanding the Link Between Dental Exams and Overall Health

Your mouth is a window to the rest of your body. Researchers have found links between gum disease and several other health conditions, which is one more reason regular dental exams matter.

Why regular dental exams matter

A dental exam does more than check for cavities. It helps us spot early signs of gum disease and other changes in your mouth, so problems can be treated while they are small, comfortable and less costly to fix.

Oral health and your overall health

  • Heart disease. Studies have linked gum disease with a higher risk of heart disease. Treating gum inflammation early is good for your mouth and may support your heart.
  • Diabetes. The relationship goes both ways. Diabetes can make gum disease more likely, and serious gum disease can make blood sugar harder to manage.
  • Breathing problems. Bacteria from the mouth can be inhaled into the lungs, which may contribute to respiratory infections in some people.
  • Pregnancy. Gum disease during pregnancy has been linked to early birth and low birth weight. Keeping up with dental care during pregnancy is important.
  • Bone health. Bone loss in the jaw can sometimes be seen on dental X-rays and may be connected to conditions like osteoporosis.

These links don't mean that gum disease causes every one of these conditions. They do mean that caring for your gums is part of caring for your whole body.

What happens during a dental exam?

At Bliss, a complete exam includes digital X-rays, a full exam of your teeth, gums and jaw, and an oral cancer screening. We review your health history and medications so we can look at your mouth in the context of your overall health, then show you what we found and explain your options in plain words.

How often should you come in?

Most people do well with a cleaning and exam twice a year. If you have gum disease or certain health conditions, we may recommend more frequent visits. See our guide to how often to get your teeth cleaned.

Overdue for an exam? Book a visit or call us, and we'll find a time that works.

FAQ

Quick answers.

Can gum disease affect the rest of my body?

Research has linked gum disease to conditions such as heart disease and diabetes. The links are about risk, not a guarantee, but they are a good reason to treat gum problems early.

Should I tell my dental team about my health conditions?

Yes. Your health history and medications help us look at your mouth in context, and we review them at every new patient visit.

What is a periodontal maintenance cleaning?

It is a deeper cleaning schedule, usually three to four times a year, for patients who have been treated for gum disease and need help keeping it under control.
