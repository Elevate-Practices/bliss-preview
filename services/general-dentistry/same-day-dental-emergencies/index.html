What Qualifies as a Dental Emergency?

A dental emergency is an oral health problem that requires timely evaluation because delaying care could increase discomfort, infection, damage, or the risk of losing a tooth.

Contact an emergency dentist if you are experiencing symptoms such as:

  • Severe or persistent tooth pain
  • Swelling of the gums, face, or jaw
  • A suspected dental abscess or infection
  • A knocked-out permanent tooth
  • A cracked, fractured, or broken tooth
  • Significant dental trauma
  • Uncontrolled bleeding from the mouth
  • A damaged crown, filling, or other restoration causing pain or exposing the tooth
  • Sudden pain when biting or chewing

Patients with severe facial swelling that affects breathing or swallowing, major facial trauma, or bleeding that cannot be controlled should seek emergency medical attention rather than relying solely on a dental-office visit.

When Is Tooth Pain a Dental Emergency?

Not every toothache requires immediate treatment, but persistent or severe pain should not be ignored. Tooth pain can result from decay, a cracked tooth, inflammation inside the tooth, infection, gum problems, or damage to an existing restoration.

If pain is intense, keeps you awake, becomes worse when biting, or occurs with swelling or fever, contact a fort worth emergency dentist for an evaluation.

Depending on the cause, treatment could involve repairing the tooth, managing an infection, performing root canal therapy, or recommending another appropriate procedure.

Dental Swelling and Infections

Swelling around a tooth or gum may indicate infection and should be assessed promptly. A dental abscess can develop when bacteria reach tissues inside or around a tooth.

Possible warning signs include:

  • Throbbing tooth pain
  • Localized gum swelling
  • Facial or jaw swelling
  • Tenderness when chewing
  • A pimple-like area on the gums
  • An unpleasant taste or drainage
  • Fever or feeling generally unwell

Dental infections do not reliably resolve without professional treatment. An examination helps determine the source of the infection and whether the tooth can be treated or whether another approach is necessary.

If swelling is rapidly increasing or interfering with breathing or swallowing, seek emergency medical care.

What Should I Do If a Tooth Is Knocked Out?

A knocked-out permanent tooth is time-sensitive. Contact a dental office immediately and follow these steps when possible:

  1. Pick up the tooth by the crown, not the root.
  2. If it is dirty, gently rinse it without scrubbing or removing attached tissue.
  3. If you can safely place it back into the socket without forcing it, do so.
  4. Otherwise, keep the tooth moist in an appropriate storage medium while traveling for care.
  5. Seek professional dental treatment as quickly as possible.

Do not attempt to reinsert a knocked-out baby tooth.

Prompt assessment provides the dentist with the best opportunity to determine whether the permanent tooth can be preserved.

What Should I Do With a Broken or Cracked Tooth?

A broken tooth may range from a small chip to a fracture that affects deeper tooth structure. Rinse your mouth gently, avoid chewing on the affected side, and save any tooth fragments you can locate.

A cold compress may help with swelling after an injury. If the tooth has a sharp edge, take care to avoid further irritation to the cheek or tongue.

Depending on the extent of damage, treatment may involve bonding, another restoration, dental crowns, root canal treatment, or extraction when a tooth cannot predictably be restored.

Dentist or Emergency Room: Where Should You Go?

Many urgent tooth problems are best evaluated by a dentist because dental offices are equipped to diagnose and treat conditions involving the teeth and surrounding oral tissues.

Contact our office for concerns such as:

  • Severe toothache
  • Broken or cracked teeth
  • Knocked-out teeth
  • Lost or damaged dental restorations
  • Localized dental swelling
  • Suspected tooth infection

Go to an emergency room or seek emergency medical assistance for potentially life-threatening conditions such as:

  • Difficulty breathing or swallowing
  • Severe or rapidly spreading facial swelling
  • Major injuries involving the face or jaw
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Significant bleeding that cannot be controlled

When you are uncertain, prioritizing immediate medical safety is appropriate.

Preventing Future Dental Emergencies

Some dental emergencies result from accidents and cannot be predicted. Others begin with problems that become more serious over time.

You can reduce avoidable risks by:

  • Scheduling regular dental examinations
  • Treating cavities and damaged restorations before they worsen
  • Contacting a dentist when persistent tooth pain begins
  • Brushing and flossing consistently
  • Wearing an appropriate mouthguard for contact sports
  • Avoiding chewing ice and other very hard objects
  • Following recommended treatment for existing dental conditions

Routine dental cleanings and exams can also help identify developing concerns before they become urgent.
